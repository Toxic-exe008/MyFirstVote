🕻️ Voter's Guide

Everything a first-time voter needs to know — how to vote, what to carry, how elections work, and why your vote matters.

🌟 First Time Voter?

Welcome! Voting for the first time is a milestone. Here is everything you need to be ready on election day.

📋

Check Your Name on the Electoral Roll

Visit voters.eci.gov.in or the Voter Helpline App to verify your name is on the electoral roll. You must be enrolled to vote.

🗺️

Find Your Polling Booth

Your polling booth is assigned based on your registered address. Check it on the ECI Voter Portal or SMS EPIC<space>YourVoterID to 1950.

🕶

Arrive on Time

Polling booths open at 7:00 AM and close at 6:00 PM. Arrive early to avoid queues. If you are in the queue before 6 PM, you will be allowed to vote.

🔐

Your Vote is Secret

Voting is done via an EVM (Electronic Voting Machine). No one can see who you voted for. Your choice is completely confidential.

🏘 What to Carry

Carry any ONE valid photo identity document accepted by the ECI:

🧳

EPIC (Voter ID Card)

The Electors' Photo Identity Card is the primary document. If you have it, always carry it.

📱

Aadhaar Card

Your Aadhaar card (physical or digital via DigiLocker / mAadhaar app) is accepted as valid ID.

🛂

Passport / Driving Licence

Your Indian passport or a driving licence issued by any RTO is a valid alternative.

🏢

Other Accepted IDs

  • PAN Card
  • MNREGA Job Card
  • Smart Card issued by RGI
  • Bank / Post Office passbook with photo
  • Central / State Govt employee ID
  • Health Insurance Smart Card (ESIC/RSBY)
  • Pension document with photo
✅ Dos & ❌ Don'ts

Follow these rules to ensure your vote counts and no issues arise at the polling station.

✅ What You SHOULD Do

  • Carry a valid photo ID document
  • Check your name and polling booth number in advance
  • Arrive early and stand in the queue patiently
  • Follow all instructions from polling officials
  • Press the EVM button firmly next to your candidate's name
  • Wait for the beep confirming your vote was recorded
  • Check the VVPAT slip to verify your vote
  • Maintain silence inside the polling station
  • Vote independently, free of any pressure or influence
  • Report irregularities to the Presiding Officer or call 1950

❌ What You MUST NOT Do

  • Do NOT carry your mobile phone into the voting booth
  • Do NOT photograph or record the EVM screen
  • Do NOT take photos or videos inside the polling station
  • Do NOT campaign or display party symbols within 200m of the booth
  • Do NOT accept money, liquor, or gifts in exchange for your vote
  • Do NOT vote more than once — it is a criminal offence
  • Do NOT impersonate another voter
  • Do NOT create any disturbance at the polling station
  • Do NOT enter the polling station armed or with any weapon
  • Do NOT reveal your vote to anyone — it is secret by law
🏛️ How India's Election System Works

As per the Election Commission of India (ECI) — a step-by-step guide to how democratic elections are conducted.

1

Announcement of Election Schedule

The Election Commission of India announces the election schedule and the Model Code of Conduct (MCC) comes into immediate effect. The MCC prohibits new government schemes and regulates campaign activities to ensure a level playing field for all parties.

2

Electoral Roll Revision & Voter Registration

The ECI updates electoral rolls before every election. Citizens who turn 18 on or before January 1 of the election year can register at voters.eci.gov.in or via Form 6 at their local Booth Level Officer (BLO). You must be an Indian citizen to register.

3

Filing of Nominations

Candidates file their nomination papers along with a self-sworn affidavit disclosing assets, liabilities, educational qualifications, and any criminal cases. These affidavits are publicly available on MyNeta.info. After scrutiny, the final list of candidates is published by the ECI.

4

Campaign Period

Candidates and parties campaign across their constituencies. All campaigns must follow the Model Code of Conduct. Spending is capped at ₹28 lakh per candidate for Maharashtra Assembly elections. The campaign period ends 48 hours before polling day (the mandatory silent period).

5

Polling Day — Your Vote

On polling day, voters visit their assigned booth. Identity is verified against the electoral roll and your left index finger is marked with indelible ink. You then press the EVM button next to your chosen candidate. A VVPAT slip flashes for 7 seconds so you can verify your choice. Your vote is secret and cannot be traced back to you.

6

Counting of Votes & Results

On counting day (1–3 days after polling), EVMs are unsealed and votes counted under strict security. India uses the First Past The Post (FPTP) system — the candidate with the most votes in a constituency wins, with no minimum percentage required. Results are declared constituency by constituency.

7

Government Formation

For Maharashtra Assembly elections, the party or alliance winning more than 145 out of 288 seats forms the government. The Governor invites the majority leader to be sworn in as Chief Minister. If no single party has a majority, coalition negotiations begin to form a hung assembly government.

8

Types of Elections in India

Lok Sabha (General Elections) — Every 5 years; 543 constituencies; elects MPs; PM is the majority leader.  |  Vidhan Sabha (State Assembly) — Every 5 years per state; elects MLAs; CM is the majority leader.  |  Local Body Elections — BMC, Gram Panchayat, Municipal Councils; conducted by the State Election Commission.  |  Rajya Sabha — Members elected by state legislators, not directly by voters.

⚖️ Important Rules to Remember

Key provisions under the Representation of the People Act, 1951 and ECI guidelines:

🔞

Age Requirement

You must be 18 years or older as on January 1 of the registration year to vote. To contest elections, the minimum age is 25 for Lok Sabha or Vidhan Sabha seats.

🖊

Indelible Ink

Your left index finger is marked with indelible ink after voting to prevent double voting. The ink stays for several days and cannot be easily washed off.

📵

No Phones in the Voting Booth

Section 128 of the RPA 1951 mandates secrecy of voting. Photographing your vote or the EVM is a criminal offence punishable with up to 1 year in prison.

🧹

NOTA Option

None of the Above (NOTA) is available on all EVMs as the last option. You can use NOTA if you do not wish to vote for any candidate. Note: NOTA votes do not affect the winner.

♿

Accessibility & PwD Voters

Persons with Disabilities (PwD) and senior citizens above 85 are entitled to Postal Ballot or home voting. All polling stations must have ramps, wheelchairs, and Braille EVMs.

🚨

Voter Helpline

Election Commission of India 24×7 helpline: 1950. Use the cVIGIL app to report MCC violations, booth capturing, or voter inducement with photo/video evidence.
